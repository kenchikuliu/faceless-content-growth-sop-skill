[header-two] What brands are actually buying
[unstyled] UGC stands for user-generated content. Short vertical videos that look like a regular person reviewing or demonstrating a product. 

Brands use them in ads, on landing pages, and as social posts because they convert dramatically better than polished commercial content.
[unstyled] The numbers brands are paying in 2026 for a single 30-second AI UGC video:
[unstyled] > Skincare and beauty: $400-800
> Supplements and wellness: $500-1,000
> Software and SaaS: $600-1,500
> DTC food and beverage: $300-700
> Fashion and apparel: $400-900
[unstyled] These are per-video rates. Brands typically order in batches of 5-10. A single batch can pay $3,000-12,000 depending on niche.
[unstyled] The reason brands pay this is simple. A real UGC creator quotes $800, takes two weeks, and might film three usable angles.

The AI version delivers ten variations in two days, every one shot to spec, no schedule conflicts, no diva behavior. The math is not close.

[header-two] Step 1: Pick a Niche That Brands Pay For
[unstyled] Not every niche has buyer-side budget. Pick one where brands actively buy UGC ads.
[unstyled] The proven money niches:
[unstyled] > Skincare and beauty (huge UGC budgets, easy AI product shots)
> Supplements and wellness (recurring brand demand, good per-video rates)
> Software and SaaS (highest per-video pay, simpler visuals)
> Home and kitchen products (volume play, lots of brands)
> Fitness equipment and apparel (big audiences, repeat buyers)

[unstyled] Avoid: anything regulated (alcohol, CBD, pharmaceuticals, financial products), anything where the product needs to be physically demoed in a way AI struggles with, anything saturated with big-name creators where brands already have deep rosters.
[unstyled] Pick one niche. Build the entire account in that niche. Mixed niches kill brand deals because brands cannot tell what audience you actually have.
