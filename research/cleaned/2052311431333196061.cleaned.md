## What brands are actually buying

UGC stands for user-generated content. Short vertical videos that look like a regular person reviewing or demonstrating a product. 

Brands use them in ads, on landing pages, and as social posts because they convert dramatically better than polished commercial content.

The numbers brands are paying in 2026 for a single 30-second AI UGC video:

> Skincare and beauty: $400-800
> Supplements and wellness: $500-1,000
> Software and SaaS: $600-1,500
> DTC food and beverage: $300-700
> Fashion and apparel: $400-900

These are per-video rates. Brands typically order in batches of 5-10. A single batch can pay $3,000-12,000 depending on niche.

The reason brands pay this is simple. A real UGC creator quotes $800, takes two weeks, and might film three usable angles.

The AI version delivers ten variations in two days, every one shot to spec, no schedule conflicts, no diva behavior. The math is not close.

Your job is to be the operator who delivers it.

## Step 1: Pick a Niche That Brands Pay For

Not every niche has buyer-side budget. Pick one where brands actively buy UGC ads.

The proven money niches:

> Skincare and beauty (huge UGC budgets, easy AI product shots)
> Supplements and wellness (recurring brand demand, good per-video rates)
> Software and SaaS (highest per-video pay, simpler visuals)
> Home and kitchen products (volume play, lots of brands)

## Step 2: Build the Account Persona

Your account needs a clear identity. Brands evaluate creators in 30 seconds. 

If they cannot tell what your account is about from three videos, they move on.

Use Claude to develop the persona:

```
I am building a faceless UGC TikTok and Red Note account in the 
[niche] space. Help me develop a clear account identity.

Generate:
1. Account name and handle that signals the niche immediately
2. A 100-character bio that tells brands what audience I serve
3. A content pillars structure, three core topic categories I will 
   rotate through
4. The tone and visual style that will feel cohesive across videos
5. Three opening hook formats I can reuse across hundreds of videos

Keep it specific to [niche]. Avoid generic creator language.
```

Claude returns a full account identity in one shot. Use it as your reference for everything you post.

## Step 3: Generate the Scripts

This is where Claude does the heavy lifting. One prompt produces a week of content.

```
You are a UGC scriptwriter who has written for top-converting DTC 
brands. Write five 30-second TikTok scripts for a faceless UGC 
account in the [niche] space.

For each script:
- Hook in the first 3 seconds, no slow buildup
- A specific problem the target audience actually has
- A product angle that introduces a solution naturally
- Visual cues for the AI presenter, what they are doing, holding, 
  showing
- A line that makes the viewer feel understood, not sold to
- Caption text overlay at the key moment
- A natural soft CTA at the end, no aggressive sales pitch

Make every script feel different. Different hook style, different 
problem, different angle. Avoid generic UGC openers like "you 
guys, I just found this" or "okay so I tried this and."

Format each script as: hook line, body, visual notes, caption, CTA.
```

You now have five scripts ready to shoot.

## Step 4: Generate the Videos in Higgsfield

This is the production step. Each script becomes a 30-second video.

For each script, generate the AI presenter footage in Higgsfield with prompts like:

```
Create a 30-second vertical UGC-style video. A young woman, 
mid-20s, casual at home setting, soft natural lighting, holding 
[product type]. She looks directly at the camera and speaks 
naturally. Authentic and conversational tone, not polished or 
commercial. Slight handheld feel, like she filmed it on her phone. 
Format 9:16, 1080x1920.
```

Adjust the presenter description to match your account's identity. Different age, ethnicity, vibe per account if you run multiple. The presenter should feel like a real person who could exist on TikTok.

For product shots specifically, generate close-ups of the product being held, used, or shown. These cut into the video as B-roll.

Stitch the presenter footage with the product B-roll using any basic editor. Add the captions Claude wrote on the key moments. Export.

A polished 30-second UGC video, end to end, takes about 20-30 minutes once your workflow is dialed in.

## Step 5: Post to TikTok and Red Note Daily

This is non-negotiable. Brands look at consistency before they look at follower count. An account posting once a week looks like a hobby. An account posting daily looks like a creator they can rely on.

Post the same video to both platforms within the same hour. Slightly different captions tuned to each platform. TikTok captions are short and punchy. Red Note captions are slightly longer with more structure, the platform rewards detail.

For the first 30 days, post one video per day on each platform. Build up a portfolio of 60+ videos across both. This is your audition.

After 30 days, brands will start finding you. Before 30 days, you start finding them.

## Step 6: Land Brand Deals

Two paths, run both.

Inbound path: Once your TikTok and Red Note accounts have 30+ videos each in a clean niche, brands will start reaching out. 

Make it easy for them. Bio link to a simple page with your rates, a contact form, and three example videos. 

The page should look professional, not like a personal portfolio.

Outbound path: This is where most operators leave money on the table. Do not wait for brands to find you, find them.

Use Claude to identify and pitch brands directly:

```
Find me 20 [niche] DTC brands actively running paid ads on TikTok 
or Instagram right now. For each one, identify:
- Brand name and website
- The kind of UGC content they currently use in ads
- One specific gap or improvement opportunity in their current ads
- The likely budget tier based on company size

Then write a personalized cold email to each one offering AI UGC 
production services. The email should:
- Reference a specific ad of theirs I noticed
- Show I understand their audience
- Offer a single test video at a reasonable rate
- Include links to my TikTok and Red Note accounts

Keep each email under 100 words. No corporate pleasantries. No 
mention of AI tools, just deliverables and results.

Format as a clean table I can paste into an outreach tool.
```

Send 10-20 of these per week. Reply rates run 5-15% if your accounts are real and your pitch is specific. Closing one in three positive replies is normal.

## Pricing and Packaging

Two ways to package your offer.

Per-video pricing: $400-1,500 depending on niche, charge a 50% deposit upfront, deliver 1-2 revisions free, anything beyond is billed.

Monthly retainer: $2,500-8,000 per month for 8-15 videos delivered. This is where the real money is. Recurring revenue, predictable workload, deeper brand relationships.

Most operators close one or two retainers and stop chasing one-off deals entirely. Three retainers at $4,000 each is $12,000 monthly recurring from one operator running one workflow.

The retainer offer is what you pitch. Mention the per-video rate as a fallback. Brands often start with one video and convert to a retainer once they see the conversion data.
